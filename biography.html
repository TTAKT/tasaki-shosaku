BiographyA chronology compiled from exhibition catalogs (1988–2002) and cross-checked where possible against independent records

Tasaki Shosaku was born in 1929 in Fukuoka Prefecture, the nephew of the painter Tazaki Hirosuke. After moving to Tokyo in 1951 he trained informally alongside two major figures of Japanese modern painting, studied under Inokuma Genichiro, and held his first solo exhibition in 1953. Over the following five decades he exhibited continuously with the Nihon-fu and Gendai-ten societies, developed two sustained bodies of work — the Buddhist Shakuson-ki series and the Silk Road Fantasy series — and in 2002 completed a 10-panel mural cycle permanently installed in the main hall of Jindaiji Temple, Tokyo.

  • 1929
    Born January 17 in Kitayama, Tachibana-machi, Yame-gun, Fukuoka Prefecture — the same family home as his uncle, painter Tazaki Hirosuke.
  • 1946
    As a middle-schooler, lived for three years with his uncle during wartime evacuation, and frequented the nearby atelier of painter Sakamoto Hanjiro.
  • 1951
    Moved to Tokyo and stayed at his uncle's residence in Nerima.
  • 1953
    Studied under Inokuma Genichiro in Denenchofu. Held his first solo exhibition, at Toden Hall, Tokyo.
  • 1954–1962
    Continued exhibiting at the Shinseisaku-ten and independent group shows; subject matter gradually shifted toward Eastern themes.
  • 1963
    Participated in founding the Western-painting division of the Nihon-fu exhibiting society.
  • 1965
    Won the Nihon-fu Prize at the 12th Nihon-fu exhibition for Gyoko (Dawn Light).
  • 1967–1968
    Began exhibiting works from the Shakuson-ki (釈尊記, “Life of Buddha”) series, which he continued to develop for the rest of his career.
  • 1972
    Won the Nihon-fu Prize for Queen of Samarkand.
  • 1973
    Won a commemorative prize at the Nihon-fu exhibition.
  • 1980
    Published an etching portfolio, Silk Road Fantasy, comprising 75 plates.
  • 1983
    Elected a member of the Gendai-ten exhibiting society.
  • 1984
    Won the members' prize at the 40th Gendai-ten exhibition for Shigan Higan (“This Shore, That Shore”), from the Shakuson-ki series.
  • 1986
    Won the Gendai-ten Prize for Shukufuku (Blessing).
  • 1988
    Solo exhibition of 30 large-format works at the Fukuoka Art Museum, organized by the Nishinippon Shimbun newspaper and supported by TV Nishinippon.
  • 1993
    40th career-anniversary retrospective in his hometown, organized by the Nishinippon Shimbun. A separate exhibition of 12 F150-size works (including Ascetic, from the Shakuson-ki series) was held the same year at Ginza Art Gallery, organized by the Tasaki Shosaku Exhibition Executive Committee together with Tomio Construction Co.
  • 1996
    Painted the Taishoji Mandala, a triptych (Gozanze Myo-o, Dainichi Nyorai, and Aizen Myo-o; each 145.5×97.0cm, oil) on commission from Taishoji Temple.
  • 1997
    Formally dedicated the Taishoji Mandala triptych to Taishoji Temple. The same year, donated Raijin (Thunder God, F80 size) to a Shinto shrine in Okayama City.
  • 1998–1999
    Continued to exhibit at Gendai-ten and held gallery exhibitions organized by the Asahi Art Gallery. Donated Sun God and Moon God to the Higashi Group Memorial Hall (1999).
  • 2000
    50th career-anniversary retrospective at the Mitaka City Art Gallery. Honorary chairman: Sakurauchi Yoshio, former Speaker of Japan's House of Representatives. Committee chair: Nakae Toshitada, former president of the Asahi Shimbun. Supported by the Mitaka arts foundation, the Nerima Board of Education, and Asahi Shimbun Jigyo, with additional sponsorship from the Imperial Hotel, the architecture firm Yamashita Sekkei, and others. Began work on the 10-panel mural for Jindaiji Temple's main hall.
  • 2001
    Produced the original artwork for a commemorative postage stamp on commission from Mongolia's Ministry of Posts, as the Japan-side artist.
  • 2002
    Completed and formally dedicated the 10-panel Shakuson-ki mural cycle for the main hall of Jindaiji Temple, Chofu, Tokyo — one of the Kanto region's oldest temples, whose principal Buddha statue (a Hakuho-era work) was later designated a National Treasure in 2017. The same year, the Ikeda 20th Century Art Museum planned a three-month exhibition, “The World of Tasaki Shosaku” (June–August).
  • 2006
    Held an exhibition themed around the world of the Shakuson-ki series.

Works are also recorded in the collection of Tsukiji Honganji Temple, Tokyo.

This chronology is compiled from exhibition catalogs produced by the artist and his family, and is periodically cross-checked against independent institutional and bibliographic records as they are located. Some details (exact dates, venue names) remain under verification and are refined as further sources are confirmed.
