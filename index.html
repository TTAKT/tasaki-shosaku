Tasaki Shosaku Japanese painter, born 1929, Fukuoka — best known for a 10-panel Life of Buddha mural cycle and the Silk Road Fantasy series

Tasaki Shosaku (田崎昭作) is a Japanese oil painter whose half-century career centered on two sustained bodies of work: the Shakuson-ki (釈尊記, “Life of Buddha”) series, and Silk Road Fantasy (シルクロード幻想), a series inspired by the historic trade route between East Asia and the Mediterranean. His best-known work is a set of ten large oil-on-canvas murals depicting the life of the Buddha, painted in 2001–2002 and permanently installed in the main hall of Jindaiji Temple (深大寺) in Chofu, Tokyo — a temple founded in the Nara period and home to a Hakuho-era Buddha statue designated a National Treasure in 2017.

He is the nephew of the noted Western-style painter Tazaki Hirosuke (田崎廣助), and as a young man he studied informally alongside two major figures of Japanese modern painting, Sakamoto Hanjiro (坂本繁二郎) and Inokuma Genichiro (猪熊弦一郎). He held his first solo exhibition in Tokyo in 1953 and went on to exhibit continuously at the Nihon-fu (日府展) and Gendai-ten (現代展) exhibiting societies for decades, while his work was also shown in solo retrospectives organized with regional and national press (Nishinippon Shimbun, Asahi Shimbun Jigyo) and supported by public cultural bodies.

Jindaiji Temple Murals (2001–2002)

Ten large oil panels retelling the life of the Buddha — Birth, Palace Life, the Four Sights, the Great Departure, Ascetic Practice, the Defeat of Mara, the First Sermon, and more, ending in the Parinirvana — permanently installed in the main hall of a 1,300-year-old Tokyo temple.

Silk Road Fantasy

A sustained series, including a 1980 etching portfolio of 75 plates, imagining the landscapes, cities and peoples of the historic Silk Road from Central Asia to the Mediterranean.

A 50-year career, publicly reviewed

His 1993 and 2000 retrospective exhibitions drew published essays from independent art critics, and the 2000 exhibition's honorary committee included a former Speaker of the House of Representatives and a former president of the Asahi Shimbun.

A working catalog of 69 titled works compiled from exhibition catalogs spanning 1966–2006, organized into the Jindaiji mural cycle, the Shakuson-ki series, and the Silk Road series.
